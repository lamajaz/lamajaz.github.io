Quién Soy... هويّتي

My identity - هويتي - is one of the most important things I carry. It travels with me everywhere I go, often without needing explanation. My name tells one story, and my languages tell another: Arabic, Spanish, English, and French. People often pause at the combination - an Arabic name spoken with a Venezuelan tone, English shaped by migration, and a curiosity that led me to French. These languages are maps of where I come from, my family’s heritage, and the many places where I’ve had to learn how to belong.

Even simple questions like “What is your favorite place?” or “How do you like to spend your day?” reveal a lot about my identity. My answer is always the same: by the water. Whether it’s the Caribbean or the Mediterranean, the ocean understands my heart in ways people sometimes cannot. My attachment to water is intertwined with who I am; I carry the Mediterranean in my blood and the Caribbean in my childhood memories of Sunday beach trips.

Being born and raised in Venezuela, with summers in Lebanon to keep us connected to that side of our family, meant growing up with a constant sense of being “in between.” I didn’t fully understand what it meant to be a third‑culture kid until college and early adulthood. I’ve spent years wondering if I’m Venezuelan enough or Lebanese enough, eventually realizing that identity doesn’t always fit neatly into one box. Even my Arabic accent carries my family’s history — a جَبَلِيّ (jabali) tone that comes from the mountains that hold my family’s lineage and stories.

Growing up between Venezuela and Lebanon meant witnessing the realities of instability, change, and rebuilding. These experiences shaped how I understand community, care, and responsibility.

These experiences taught me to never let go of my humanity or empathy. They shaped my commitment to community healing, care, and the belief that even small acts of support matter. They also gave me a love for spontaneity, change, and courage — a desire to seek adventure, chase dreams, and do things even when fear is present. Al vértigo, alas.

All of this shapes how I engage with others: with empathy, curiosity, cultural humility, and a deep respect for the stories people carry. My identity is layered, complex and constantly evolving, and I try to show up in the world in a way that honors every part of it.
